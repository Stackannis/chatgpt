# Critiques en attente de correction (gauntlet, tour unique, critiques Opus 5.5)


## critic:bg — FAIL

1. layers[5] (near jungle, 768x128): the canopy is about 80 near-identical diagonal 'scale' blobs of the same size, with the same 3-tone step everywhere from row ~40 to the bottom. It reads as a noise texture, not painted foliage. There are no big masses, no rhythm of near and far clumps, and no lit crowns against shadowed gaps. In play-bg-b it fills the bottom half of the screen as a busy, flat green carpet. Fix: group the canopy into 4-6 large rounded tree-crown masses per 384px. Give each crown a lit upper-left rim (1-2 light rows) over a mid body, separate crowns with darker hollow pockets, and let the bottom ~40 rows fall off into one quiet dark tone with sparse dither. That gives the eye a resting area behind the terrain.
2. layers[5]: this layer sits right behind the action but is too saturated and too close in value to the foreground greens. The background palms (green fronds, ~1px stems) and the round broadleaf tree read as foreground-ish props, most of all in play-bg-b where they stand between the floating platforms next to the real foreground palm. Fix: push the whole layer toward blue-teal haze. Lift the darkest value by about 2 steps and cut saturation by about 25%. Make the palm fronds the same hazy teal as the canopy top, not a brighter green. Put no bright yellow-green anywhere in this layer, so the foreground palms, bushes and grass are the only saturated greens on screen.
3. layers[5] palms: 7-8 palms of nearly identical size and frond shape, evenly spaced along the ridge, with flat grey-mauve trunks one pixel wide that lean and step as a jagged staircase. They look stamped and toothpick-thin at 1x. Fix: use 3 sizes (one or two tall hero palms per tile and smaller clusters of 2-3), vary the frond silhouettes (drooping, windswept, a spread fan), and make the trunks 2px with a light left and dark right edge in the hazy palette, curving smoothly instead of in single-pixel stair jumps.
4. layers[4] (islands and waterfall cliff, 1024x152): the lower ~55% is a flat untextured mint fill with a few random 1px dashes. It shows plainly when the camera is raised (demo frame 4, and the play-bg-b sea area), where it looks unfinished next to the rich water in layer 2 and layer 3. Fix: carry the lagoon down in horizontal bands that get darker and more saturated toward the viewer (2-3 hue-shifted teal steps with ordered-dither transitions). Add glitter dashes that grow longer and sparser further down, and reflected island and cliff colour under the islands. Then give the layer raster: ~2 so the bands line-scroll.
5. layers[4] mid hills: the large light-green hill faces use a high-frequency 50% checkerboard dither over big areas. At 1x it shimmers against the soft canopy and is the highest-contrast texture in the backdrop, which pulls attention away from the play area (centre of play-bg-b, right of play-bg-c). Fix: keep dither only in narrow transition bands between two tones (Sonic 3 style). Fill the open hill faces with solid ramp tones and model the shading as hill-shaped forms (lit upper-left slopes, darker right flanks), not uniform dither.
6. layers[4] waterfall cliff: the waterfalls are a single static column with evenly spaced vertical dashes and a white blob splash, and the rock is smooth lavender slabs with a few scratch lines, so it reads as a placeholder next to the painterly clouds. Fix: give the rock real faceting (lit top planes, mid faces, a darker cool shadow side, a few crack clusters), make the falls 2-3 tone streams that widen at the bottom, and paint a layered foam and mist spray, with a faint mist dither haloing over the lagoon.
7. layers[0]/layers[1] clouds: the big cumulus are built from stacked same-sized round lumps, each lit the same way (white cap, lavender underside), which gives a pillow and bubble look. The two cloud layers also share one design language and scale, so they barely read as different depths. Fix: on the big clouds, vary lump size strongly (a large dominant tower plus small satellite puffs), add a flat darker base shelf, and let the shadow core wrap through the interior gaps. Make layer 0 clouds smaller, flatter and paler, closer to the sky colour, so the near layer clearly sits in front.
8. layers[2] (far sea and mountains): the distant mountains are tiny and mostly hidden behind the cloud layer, and the sea's 1px white sparkles are scattered evenly at random over the whole band, which reads as noise rather than horizon glitter. Fix: make 1-2 far island and mountain silhouettes larger and recognisable (pale blue, 2 tones, a snow or light-face highlight on the upper left). Concentrate the glitter in a brighter band just under the horizon and thin it out toward the bottom, using horizontal 2-4px dashes rather than single dots.

À préserver : Keep the overall structure: 6 layers in a clean far-to-near order, the banded dithered tropical sky, good atmospheric colour grading, no gaps even with the camera raised 200px, and contract and tiling are clean. The cumulus scale and the blue-sea and turquoise-lagoon palette already set a convincing Sonic 3 / DKC mood, and the foreground does pop in most shots.

## critic:terrain — FAIL

1. dirt (64x64), the biggest problem on screen: every row of slabs has the same height and the same wave, so the 64px repeat shows in every screenshot (play-terrain-a/c/d look like wallpaper with identical stacked chevrons). Each slab is one flat tone with a thin light line on top, so it reads as stamped pattern, not stylised earth. Fix: vary the strata heights (one tall band, two thin ones), offset the waves so no row lines up with the next, give each slab a 2-step form (lit top-left corner, a 1px darker purple-brown shade along the bottom-right under the dark seam), and add 2-3 larger embedded features per tile (a rounded rock cluster, a fossil/root, a 2-tone pebble group) so the eye stops seeing the grid.
2. dirt to dirtDeep depth transition: in-game this shows as blotchy, noisy checker clouds (play-terrain-a around the 45-degree hill base, play-terrain-d left half, demo cliff right side), the worst 'AI-noise' tell on screen. dirtDeep already shares dirt's geometry. Fix inside terrain.js: shift dirtDeep's ramp only 1 step darker and more purple than dirt, matched colour for colour, and drop dirt's brightest highlight colour from dirtDeep entirely. The ordered dither then reads as a smooth tonal band, not salt-and-pepper patches.
3. grass (64x24): the blades above row 8 are a regular sawtooth repeating about every 8px, the mat is one flat lime with almost no mid or dark clumps, and the lower fringe is a uniform small zigzag. There is no real dark cast shadow on the dirt under the fringe, so grass and dirt meet with no depth (very visible on the flats in play-terrain-a/c). Fix: build the mat from 3-4 big rounded clumps of uneven width (Yoshi's Island/DKC style) with a lit yellow-green top-left, a mid green, and a teal-shifted dark green core. Vary the fringe drop from 2 to 7px with rounded tips, and add a 1-2px dark purple-brown shadow row under the fringe on rows 21-23. Cut the blades to 3-4 irregular tufts per 64px.
4. wallL / wallR (8x64): wallL is a thin cream stripe with dark horizontal ticks that don't line up with the dirt's slab seams, so the cliff faces in the demo and play-terrain-c read as a pale line with tally marks, not a rounded rock edge. wallR is just a hard ink band. Fix: make a 3-4px bevel ramp (highlight, light, base, then the dirt) with the outline at x=0, and notches that follow the dirt seam positions (the dirt pattern repeats vertically on the same 64px period). On wallR use a 2-3px dark purple shade before the outline at x=7, so the right side reads as turned away from the light.
5. under (64x8): floating islands end in a flat dark strip, so the island in the demo looks like a sawn-off brick. Fix: give the underside an irregular silhouette, with rounded lumps, a couple of 2-4px hanging roots or moss drips, a dark shaded band and the ink outline on row 7, so islands look rounded and grown, not cut.
6. spikes (16x16): only 2 blunt, bullet-shaped cones with rounded tips and big gaps. They are grey on grey, have no base, and at 1x read as harmless stubs, not danger. Fix: 3 (or 4) sharp cones with 1px tips, a hue-shifted steel ramp (blue-violet shade, white-yellow highlight on the left face), a dark metal or stone base strip on rows 13-15, and a full ink outline so they pop against the sky and the grass.
7. goal (4 frames 32x48): the pillar glyphs are a jumble of small square spirals that turn to mush at 1x, the grass clumps on the lintel read as green blobs, and the light column is noisy speckle instead of a clean animated band. Fix: one big readable sun or spiral glyph per pillar block, carved with a 2-tone bevel. Use 2-3 crisp vertical light bands that scroll upward between frames, and replace the blobs with shaped leaf clusters or vines hanging from the lintel.
8. spring (3 frames 16x24): in the extended frame the coil is a stack of identical grey rungs that reads as a ladder or column, and the idle frame's coil is flat with little volume. Fix: draw the coil as alternating zigzag turns with a lit front and dark back (cylindrical 3-step shade), squash and stretch the turn spacing clearly between frames, and keep the base plate pixel-identical across all 3 frames. Also give the red cap a stronger highlight cluster in the upper left.

À préserver : Keep the palette, which coheres with the backdrop (warm red-brown earth, saturated grass), and the clean ink outlines on the props. The sun-face prize block, the glowing checkpoint lantern and the log platform are appealing, readable and close to shippable. Keep the contract: all 67 assets present, and no terrain errors in tools/contract.js.

## critic:enemies — FAIL

1. flyer.fly (all 4 frames): the bird is mid/dark blue with a navy outline and flies against a blue sky and sea, so in play-enemies-c it is the lowest-contrast sprite on screen and nearly disappears when it passes over the sea band. Change the main hue to one the backdrop doesn't use: warm magenta/coral, or a purple matching the walker family, with a yellow or cream belly. Keep the hue-shifted ramp (light pink-orange highlight, wine-purple shadow) and use a dark wine outline instead of navy so the silhouette stands out from both the sky and the green foliage.
2. walker.walk and spiker.walk: these aren't leg cycles. The 4 frames are the same body with the yellow hoof blocks moved up 1px in turn, and the spiker's feet barely change at all, so in motion both enemies look like they shuffle in place. Draw real contact / down / passing / up poses with front and back leg pairs moving opposite each other (one pair forward and planted while the other lifts 2px and swings back). Add a 1px body drop on the contact frames, plus a matching ear/tail and spike-shell bounce, so the bob comes from the legs.
3. walker.walk and squash, spiker.walk, flyer.fly: pixels run into the frame edges. The walker's curly tail sits on x=23, the spiker's top spike tip sits on y=0 and its back spike on x=23, and the flyer's beak, tail and wing tips touch x=0, x=23 and y=0. At 1x and in motion the silhouettes look cropped, and the outline is missing where they meet the edge. Keep at least 1px of transparent margin on every side apart from the bottom contact row: pull the tail and wings inward and shorten or lower the spikes by 1-2px.
4. spiker.walk: the body is a flat red rectangle slab with a straight horizontal white band, a boxy brick with spikes on top and no form. Rebuild the shell as a rounded dome: a curved top silhouette, a specular highlight upper-left, a 3-step ramp from red to dark crimson to wine down to a curved rim, and a scalloped or segmented rim instead of the straight white line. Seat the spikes radially on the dome (outer spikes tilted outward) and shade them with an upper-left light: bright face, grey-violet shadow side, dark tip outline. Then 'do not jump on me' reads at 1x from the shape alone.
5. walker and spiker heads, eyes: the eye is a flat white square with a black block pupil and no outline-to-sclera transition or brow, so the faces look blank and identical, without the personality the brief asks for. Give each enemy a distinct, expressive eye: the walker a determined angled brow (1px dark line over the eye) and a 2-tone pupil with a 1px catchlight; the spiker a grumpy half-lidded eye. Make sure the eye shape changes very slightly across the walk frames (blink on 1 frame of the 4, or a pupil shift) for life.
6. walker.squash: the squashed sprite is a long, eyeless flat strip with the ear and tail still sticking up, and it fills the full 24px width, so it reads as a different object instead of the same boar flattened. Keep it about the same width as the walk body plus 2-3px of spread, add the classic X or spiral 'dazed' eyes and a slightly open mouth, flatten the ear and tail, and push the hooves out sideways so the stomp lands as a gag.
7. walker.walk body shading: the purple ramp is laid down in diagonal stair-stepped bands across the whole body, which looks like banding instead of a round barrel form. The shadow also doesn't wrap under the belly, so the body looks flat from the snout to the hindquarters. Re-shade it as a sphere lit from the upper-left: a soft highlight cluster on the upper shoulder, the mid tone across the middle, and a dark violet core shadow curving along the belly and rump plus a 1px reflected-light rim along the bottom. Clean up the single-pixel staircase steps on the back contour so it reads as one smooth arc.
8. flyer.fly animation: the wing poses differ, but the body stays at the same height in all frames, so the flap has no weight. Bob the body 1-2px against the wing beat (body up on the downstroke frame, down on the upstroke), keep the head and body volume identical between frames (frame 3's body/wing mass currently looks thinner than frames 1-2), and add a downstroke frame where the wing tip clearly passes below the belly line so the 4-frame cycle reads as a full flap and not a wing toggle.

À préserver : The basic designs work and should stay. The purple boar walker, the red spiked-shell spiker and the bird flyer have clear, charming concepts, dark coloured outlines and a palette that ties the walker and spiker together. The spiker's white spikes already signal danger, and the walker's purple stands out well from the green and brown terrain.

## critic:decor — FAIL

1. palm (hero piece, the largest decor sprite on screen): the fronds are a speckled mid/dark-green dither with one lime midrib line each. No leaflets are drawn, so every frond reads as a fuzzy green blob and the crown is a flat fan with no depth. Fix: hand-draw each frond as a midrib with clean saw-tooth leaflets (2-3 px strokes in alternating light/dark pairs, ending in pointed tips). Put the light fronds upper-left, the back fronds in the darkest green, and droop the lower fronds over the trunk to give the crown volume. Add a coconut cluster (2-3 brown balls with a highlight) at the crown join, and remove all single-pixel checker noise from the leaves.
2. waterfall (4 frames 32x32, hero piece at worldX 3700-3800): the in-game column is a flat mid-blue slab with evenly scattered vertical dashes. It has no edge treatment and no sense of volume, so it looks like wallpaper (shot play-decor-d). Fix: shade it as a cylinder, with darker blue at both side edges, a bright 2-3 px light band on the left third and a soft cream highlight streak. Draw long continuous falling streaks of varied length (not short dashes) that move 8 px per frame for 32 px over 4 frames, so the loop reads as fast-falling water. Add a ragged, slightly translucent outer edge so the column does not end in a ruler-straight vertical line against the cliff.
3. waterfallTop (4 frames 32x16): the lip is a straight horizontal pale bar, and in-game you can barely see any lip where the water leaves the cliff (play-decor-c, play-decor-d). Fix: draw a curved bright 'roll-over' crest where the water bulges outward, with a white-cyan highlight that thins into the falling streaks. Animate small foam bumps that travel along the crest in each frame.
4. splash (4 frames 48x16): it is one cream cloud blob that only shifts a few pixels per frame, with 1-px floating dots as spray. It reads as a static cloud stuck at the base (play-decor-b). Fix: give the 4 frames distinct poses, with the foam mound rising and collapsing and droplet arcs (2x2 droplets, not single pixels) thrown up and out to both sides and falling back. Add a light-blue shadow layer inside the foam so it has volume, and make the bottom edge blend into the water surface colour.
5. totem (24x48): it is two stacked grey boxes with rectangular slots. The fox/sun face cannot be read at 1x, and the shading is a uniform grey with a darker rim (pillow-like). Fix: carve a clear fox head silhouette (pointed ears breaking the top outline, an eye socket pair, a snout ridge) and a sun disc with rays on the lower block. Light it from the upper-left with a hue-shifted ramp (warm grey highlights, blue-violet shadows), add chipped corners so the outline is not a perfect rectangle, and let moss sit on the top-facing ledges only.
6. tree (56x72) and bush1/bush2/fern: the foliage is cauliflower lumps filled with checker dither, and bush2's red dots look like noise. The fern reads as a lettuce, not a fern. Fix: build the canopy from 4-6 clearly separated leaf clusters, each with a lit upper-left rim, a mid tone and a dark under-shadow, with dark gaps between clusters. Drop the per-pixel dithering. Give bush2 real 2x2 berries with a highlight. Draw the fern as 4-5 arching fronds with comb leaflets, matching the palm's frond language.
7. rock1/rock2: these are flat grey planes with a couple of crack lines and an even dark border. rock2's moss is a flat lime cap pasted on top. Fix: sculpt facets with a 4-5 step hue-shifted ramp (highlight plane upper-left, a strong cast-shadow side at the lower right), add a dark contact shadow at the base, and break up the moss into drip shapes that follow the top facets. The current grey is also the same value as the totem and pillar; warm it slightly so the rocks separate from the ruin stone.
8. water.surface (4 frames 32x8) and pillar/ruinBlock: the surface crest frames differ only slightly, so the waves barely move in-game. The ruinBlock spiral is a perfect machine-drawn square spiral, and the pillar is a symmetric grey tube with no damage. Fix: give the surface a clear travelling crest (shift the wave profile 8 px per frame, with sparkle pixels appearing and disappearing). Hand-carve the ruinBlock glyph with worn, broken segments and chipped edges. Break the pillar top into a jagged fracture with fallen chunks and add fluting lit from the upper-left.

À préserver : Keep the overall palette and saturation, which sit well against the backdrop and the terrain. Also keep the palm's trunk (ringed segments, warm shading, good curve) and the sunflower and pink flower, which are the strongest pieces. All 67 assets pass the contract check; the failures it reports are in the items module, not decor.

## critic:items — FAIL

1. sparkle (all 4 frames): built from 1px pale-yellow and white lines with no dark rim, so over the white clouds and the bright sky it disappears at 1x. Frame 3 breaks into scattered single dots that read as noise. Fix: give it a 2px core with a 1px orange or ink rim. Animate small + -> big + -> rotated x/star -> small +, and fade by shrinking instead of scattering. Each frame needs a solid centre that still shows over white.
2. splash frames 1-4: the crown is made of thin 1px blue-outlined 'spaghetti' fingers plus many lone 1-2px droplets. At 1x it looks like noise and the column is mostly flat cream. Fix: use 3-5 chunky droplets of 2-3px, each with a dark-blue/light-cyan/white ramp lit from the upper left. Keep the column a solid tapered mass with a darker right side, and let the droplets arc and fall over the frames instead of multiplying.
3. bigCoin: the rim silhouettes are uneven. Frame 1 has a lumpy left edge and irregular outline steps, frame 5 has a bulging, too-thick right rim, and frame 2's star is squashed out of shape. Fix: use symmetric ellipse widths per frame (about 22/18/12/4/12/18) with even stair-step outlines. Shift the rim thickness smoothly to the trailing side, keep the star a clean scaled version of frame 0, and give the edge-on frame 3 a groove/highlight stripe so it reads as a thick coin edge, not a plain bar.
4. gem (6 frames): the spin reads as a width squash, not a turning faceted shard. The bright facet stays centred, frame 1's right outline is jagged/broken, and frame 5 is basically frame 0 recoloured. Fix: move the highlight ridge and the light/dark facet split across the shape from left to right as it turns, so frames 4-5 show the darker back facets on the left. Clean up frame 1's outline so the narrowing is symmetric.
5. logo: the letter faces are flat horizontal colour bands with no real bevel, so it reads as a striped flat sticker rather than chunky bevelled 16-bit type. Fix: add a 1-2px light bevel on the top-left inner edge and a darker orange bevel on the bottom-right inner edge of every stroke, plus 1-2 white specular glints. The fox tail reads like a feather or flame and the swoosh underneath is a flat ribbon: give the tail a fluffy outline with fur notches and continue its ramp into the swoosh. The sun is pale and small, with flat orange triangle rays: strengthen its ramp and make the rays bevelled.
6. poof: frame 0 is a yellow sparkle star that looks like the collect sparkle, so the enemy-defeat burst reads as 'collected item'. Frame 1's big cloud has streaky pillow-style internal lines. Fix: make frame 0 a white impact flash with an ink-tinted rim. Shade frame 1 as distinct lobes, each lit from the upper left with a darker grey-lavender underside, so it stays one mass before it breaks into the puff ring of frames 2-3.
7. dust: frame 3 breaks into floating square specks (noise), and the tan puffs have no darker underside or outline, so they look like flat orange blobs on the brown and green terrain. Fix: shade each puff with a light top and a darker brown bottom. Fade out by shrinking and lifting 2-3 smaller round puffs instead of square confetti.
8. hud.heartEmpty: it is just a dark-red filled heart, so at a glance next to the full hearts it reads as a dark heart, not an empty slot. Fix: keep the ink outline and make the inside clearly hollow, for example a dark navy/grey interior with a faint inner rim, similar to how coinOff contrasts with coinOn.

À préserver : Must keep: the font (very legible at 1x on both sky and dirt, with a nice yellow/white face and drop shadow), the overall logo composition (sun behind, drop-shadow extrusion, tail flourish) and the HUD coinOn/coinOff pair, which is clearly readable. The gem and heart colours fit the level palette well in-game.
